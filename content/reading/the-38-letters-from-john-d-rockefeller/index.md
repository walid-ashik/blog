## My highlights

### On action

- **Do the hard thing first.** The more difficult or unpleasant a task is, the sooner you should start it. Waiting only makes it harder and scarier, like aiming a gun for so long that you never pull the trigger.
- **There's never enough time.** There will always be more to do than time to do it. Worrying about all of it only freezes you, so pick the work that matters and finish it. *(p. 20)*
- **"Later" means "never".** Saying tomorrow, next week or someday is the same as saying it won't get done. Stop waiting for perfect conditions and start now. *(p. 21)*
- **Ideas are cheap. Doing them is rare.** The world is full of people with ideas but short on people who can carry a good one through. One idea put into action beats a thousand thought up at home. *(p. 21)*
- **Habits cut both ways.** Good habits are hard to build but easy to keep. Bad habits are the opposite. *(p. 22)*
- **Life is a battle you win by acting.** Act, act again, and keep acting. That's the only real safety. *(p. 22)*
- **Strike while the iron's hot.** Great opportunities don't wait for anyone's approval. You have to work hard to grab them, and every "no" moves you closer to a "yes".
- **The secret to using time is planning.** Everyone knows a good life depends on how you use your time, yet most people waste it while getting angry when someone else takes it. Time is money, and more than that, it's your life.

### On failure, hardship and patience

- **Failure is fine. Just don't make it a habit.** *(Letter 7)*
- **Same situation, different eyes.** Optimists find opportunity in suffering. Pessimists find suffering in opportunity. His formula for success: Dream + Failure + Challenge. *(p. 34)*
- **Never struggling is the real bad luck.** Hardship builds character and the ability to get things done. People who have never faced misfortune are the unfortunate ones. *(p. 64)*
- **Fight your own weaknesses.** In a world chasing profit, you have to fight your weaknesses and anyone trying to build their happiness on your pain. *(Letter 10)*
- **Endure what others can't.** Only then can you do what others can't. *(Letter 26, p. 116)*
- **Stay calm when your pride is hurt.** When his partner's investor tried to take his name off the company, he was furious but kept his head, because there was still a long road ahead. Three years later the investor was out and the Clark-Rockefeller name was back. *(p. 117)*
- **Patience is a strategy.** It isn't swallowing your anger or being humbled. It's a deliberate choice, and it trains your character. *(p. 117)*
- **Don't sit and mourn your fate.** Wise people don't waste time complaining about bad luck.
- **Commitment matters more than mistakes.** Most people and companies fail not because they make mistakes, but because they never fully commit. *(p. 131)*

### On ambition, risk and money

- **Without ambition, nothing great happens.** He took this from his friend Henry Ford. *(Letter 19, p. 86)*
- **Second place is the same as last.** *(Letter 19, p. 86)*
- **Plan to succeed, or you're planning to fail.** People are made for a purpose, and there's no third option. *(p. 87)*
- **The size of your thinking sets the size of your success.** Success isn't measured by height, education or family background. It's measured by how big you think.
- **Higher risk, greater return.** For him, business was life's greatest adventure. *(p. 91)*
- **Money is like manure.** Spread it around and it makes things grow. Hoard it and it just stinks. He split with his cautious partner Clark because Clark was afraid of failure and never understood this. *(p. 92)*
- **He bet money, but gambled his life.** Buying out Clark cost him half the agency's shares and $72,500. What he got was freedom: he became his own boss, and no short-sighted people stood in his way. *(pp. 93–94)*
- **Playing it safe won't make you rich.** If you want the reward, you have to accept the risks that come with it. *(p. 94)*
- **Greed drove him.** He went from a bookkeeper on five dollars a week to the richest man in America, and he credits his greed for it. In his view, success achieved through greed is not a sin. *(pp. 50–51)*
- **Brains and hard work made him rich.** He became rich through a sharp mind and a strong career, and his money is clean. *(p. 52)*
- **He didn't love money. He loved making it.** *(Letter 12, p. 53)*
- **Handouts are the wrong kind of help.** Giving money away for nothing makes people lazy and takes away their dignity, and with it their chance to shape their own future.

### On people and competition

- **Today's friends can be tomorrow's enemies.** In business, people look out for themselves first. *(p. 46)*
- **His rules for dealing with people.** Only show your feelings when it helps you. Let your opponent teach you, but never teach your opponent what you know. Think twice, never act without full consideration, and take responsibility for yourself. *(p. 46)*
- **Be wary of people who ask for "sincerity".** Often they just want to benefit from you. *(Letter 10, p. 47)*
- **Stay alert.** Winning a competition depends most on staying alert. The fight starts the moment you notice an opponent trying to weaken you. *(Letter 5, p. 26)*
- **Know your opponent.** Study their strengths, weaknesses and habits, and use that knowledge to build your advantage. That's how he beat Carnegie. *(p. 130)*
- **Being underestimated is an asset.** When nobody sees you as a threat, you're building capital for the fight ahead. *(p. 130)*
- **Find the weakness first and strike hard.** In a competition, the first person to spot the other side's weakness usually wins. Go after victory with full force, but within moral limits. *(p. 131)*
- **Ignore people who say you can't.** Treat their doubt as a challenge to prove them wrong, and keep negative people away from your plans.

### On cooperation and leadership

- **Treat others the way you want to be treated.** *(Letter 23)*
- **Business can lead to friendship, not the other way round.** A friendship that grows out of business is far better than a business built on friendship. *(Letter 23)*
- **Be kind on the way up.** You'll meet the same people again on the way down.
- **You can't do big things alone.** Big achievements depend on other people's strength. To lead people well, first do the small jobs yourself so you know what it feels like to be the one taking orders. *(p. 64)*
- **Two kinds of smart people.** Some succeed by using their own talent: artists, scholars, actors. Others succeed by bringing out the talent in other people: managers and leaders. *(pp. 64–65)*
- **A boss is like a conductor.** A great employer gets every musician playing together, and the music that comes out makes money. Employers who don't treat their people well never get there. *(Letter 35, p. 155)*

### On humility, ideas and the mind

- **Play dumb on purpose.** Someone who acts smart is a fool, and someone who knows how to play the fool is smart. The smarter you are, the more it pays to stay humble, the way ripe rice bends lower the heavier it gets. *(Letter 14)*
- **"Let me wait before talking."** His rule in business: think calmly before deciding, and once you've decided, act without hesitation.
- **To find the best idea, have lots of ideas.** *(Letter 28)*
- **Believe you can do it better.** The biggest success goes to people who believe things can always be done better. *(Letter 28)*
- **Convince yourself first.** Before you can sell anything to anyone, even your soul, you have to sell it to yourself. *(Letter 37)*
